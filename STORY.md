# Story brief: The Letters She Cannot Read

**Narration language:** Amharic. **Subtitles:** English. **Length:** 60–90 seconds. **Format:** vertical first.

## Logline
A mother in a rural village who cannot read keeps every letter from her child in a tin box, until the child comes home and gives her a way to answer in her own voice.

## Treatment
In a highland village there is one big warka tree where news arrives. A mother who never went to school receives letters from her child in the city. She cannot read them, so she carries each one to the tree, where the neighbour's daughter, Selam, reads it aloud twice while the mother listens with her eyes closed, as if her child were speaking. She keeps every letter in an old tin box: twelve years of them. But her love could only travel one way; she could never write back. This year the child comes home with a simple phone and shows her one button: press, speak, let go. The phone goes into the tin box beside the letters, and now, every morning, the child hears her voice.

## Beats
1. The village and the tree where news arrives.
2. The mother cannot read, but treasures every letter.
3. The ritual: Selam reads, the mother listens.
4. The tin box: twelve years of letters, and no way to answer.
5. The return, and one button.
6. Her voice travels back.

## Characters
- **The mother:** late fifties, white netela with a green-yellow-red border, patient, warm. The only face shown.
- **Selam:** about twelve, plaits, blue school uniform; seen from behind or in profile.
- **The narrator (grown child):** never shown except from behind or as hands.

## Look and sound
Warm hand-painted illustration, soft natural light, earthy colours. Slow, single movements. A quiet masinko and krar score with no strong melody under the voice, lifting gently at the return.
